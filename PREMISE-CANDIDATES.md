# Premise Candidates — Round 1

**Status: PROPOSALS ONLY. None is canon or selected.**

We are comparing different story engines before choosing the hero.

## A. The World That Will Not Stay Mapped
The geography of the world changes in a vast cycle. Routes and places appear, move, or vanish, making exploration a race against change. Mystery: is the shifting world natural, or part of an ancient system? Risk: moving geography could feel arbitrary unless governed by clear rules.

## B. The Places the World Forgot
Some places are erased from memory, records, and accepted history, leaving contradictory ruins and traces. Explorers try to restore what was lost. Mystery: who or what erases places, and why? Risk: memory erasure can feel abstract without concrete rules and human consequences.

## C. The Impossible Arrivals
At irregular intervals, objects, creatures, messages, or survivors arrive from places no one has reached, sometimes bearing evidence of impossible civilizations or future events. Each arrival sparks an adventure while revealing a connected cause. Risk: it could become a string of disconnected episodes unless the central mystery is planned.

## D. The World Beneath the Known World
The familiar world is only one part of a larger connected reality, with hidden environments, civilizations, and natural laws. Each expedition changes what people think the world is. Risk: hidden worlds are familiar, so the structure and truth must be genuinely distinctive.

## E. The Expedition That Changed Everything
A legendary expedition achieved something that changed history, but its surviving evidence contradicts every accepted account. Rival groups launch new expeditions to uncover what really happened. Risk: it could feel like a conventional lost-treasure quest unless the discovery is truly consequential.

## First-pass comparison
- A offers expansive physical adventure but needs strict rules.
- B offers emotional mystery but needs tangible consequences.
- C offers flexible film hooks but needs a strong connecting spine.
- D offers enormous world-building but needs a fresh structure.
- E offers a clear saga mystery but needs a distinctive discovery.

## Selection test
A leading premise must support a complete first-film adventure, distinct sequels, fair clues, a specific answer to the central mystery, emotional stakes, memorable imagery, and a satisfying ending. We may combine ingredients, but should not choose until the leading options are developed enough to compare.

**Decision:** No candidate selected. Hero remains undecided.
