# Mystery and Clues

**Status:** OPEN — central mystery not yet chosen.

The saga needs a mystery with a real underlying answer, not a sequence of arbitrary twists. The answer should connect to the world and matter emotionally as well as intellectually.

## Mystery architecture

Once the central premise is chosen, define:
1. **The truth:** what actually happened or is actually happening.
2. **The public story:** what most people believe.
3. **Competing explanations:** what different groups believe and why.
4. **The evidence:** physical traces, records, locations, testimony, phenomena, and contradictions.
5. **The concealment:** why the truth is unknown or misunderstood.
6. **The stakes:** what changes if the truth is discovered.
7. **The reveal sequence:** what the audience learns in each film.
8. **The emotional meaning:** why the truth matters to the characters.
9. **The final payoff:** how the central mystery and character arcs resolve.

## Rules

- Decide the underlying truth before finalizing the reveal sequence.
- Clues should be fair, specific, and possible to notice.
- Red herrings should have an explanation, not exist solely to trick the audience.
- A reveal should recontextualize evidence, not erase it.
- Keep audience knowledge distinct from character knowledge.
- Track unresolved questions and make sure each receives a deliberate payoff or is intentionally left open.
